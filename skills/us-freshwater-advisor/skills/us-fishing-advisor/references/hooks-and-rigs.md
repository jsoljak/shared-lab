# Hooks and Rigs

Read this whenever TACKLE mode or TRIP PLAN step 8 needs a specific hook and rig,
not just a bait category. This is standard, widely-taught tackle practice — treat it
as practitioner-consensus rather than the evidence-tiered biology in
`evidence-tiers.md`; there's real regional/individual variation in exact hook-size
preference, and this gives a sound default, not the only correct answer.

Hook sizes below follow the standard convention: smaller number = bigger hook up to
1, then it flips to 1/0, 2/0, 3/0... going up (a 5/0 is bigger than a 1/0). Barbless
or crimped-barb is worth mentioning for anything likely to be released.

---

## Bass (largemouth & smallmouth)

- **Texas-rigged soft plastic (worm, creature bait):** offset worm hook, 3/0-5/0 for
  a 6-7" worm, sized down to 2/0-3/0 for smaller creature baits. Thread the hook
  point back into the plastic so it rides weedless — standard rig for fishing
  cover.
- **Jig + trailer:** the jig itself carries the hook (usually 3/0-5/0 built into a
  1/4-3/8 oz head); trailer is glued or hooked on, not a separate rig.
- **Drop shot (finesse, post-front or cold-water):** size 1-1/0 octopus or drop-shot-
  specific hook, tied with a Palomar knot leaving a tag end down to the weight —
  the hook stands off the line horizontally so the bait hovers above bottom. This is
  the go-to rig for the "downsize and slow down" post-cold-front response.
- **Live bait (nightcrawler, minnow) under a slip bobber:** size 1-1/0 baitholder or
  circle hook, hooked once through the collar (minnow) or twice through the body
  (crawler) so it stays lively.

## Panfish — crappie and bluegill

- **Jig + minnow (cold water/early season):** size 1-4 (small end) jighead, tip with
  a live minnow hooked lightly through the lips — this is the scent-plus-profile
  hedge described in `evidence-tiers.md` §9.
- **Plain jig + tube/grub (warming water):** size 4-8 light-wire jighead, no live
  bait needed once fish are actively chasing.
- **Bluegill on worm/wax worm:** size 6-10 long-shank hook (long shank makes
  unhooking small fish faster), light wire so it doesn't kill the presentation's
  subtlety.
- **Trophy bluegill on minnow/small spoon:** per `species.yaml`, larger bluegill
  shift toward minnows as they grow — size 1-4 hook or a small spoon with the
  factory treble swapped for a single open-eye (Siwash-style) hook, a documented
  guide technique that improves hookup ratio on bigger fish specifically.

## Common Carp

- **Hair rig (the standard carp-fishing rig, imported from European methodology):**
  a size 4-8 wide-gape hook tied with the hook point exposed and the bait (boilie,
  corn) mounted on a short "hair" of line below the bend rather than directly on the
  hook — the fish sucks in the bait, the hook catches the lip on the take/turn
  rather than needing to be swallowed. This is the single most important carp-
  specific rig; a plain baited hook works but the hair rig materially improves
  hookup consistency and is what US carp organizations (American Carp Society, Carp
  Anglers Group) teach as the default.
- **Simple bottom rig (corn, dough, nightcrawler):** size 6-10 baitholder hook,
  small splitshot or sliding sinker above a swivel — entry-level rig, works, just
  less refined than a hair rig for pressured fish.

## Catfish — channel, blue, flathead (do not treat as one rig)

- **Channel cat — cut bait or stink/dough bait:** size 4/0-6/0 circle hook (circle
  hooks reduce deep/gut-hooking, worth using since channel cat often swallows
  bait), fished on a slip-sinker (Carolina-style) rig so the fish can move off with
  the bait without feeling resistance before the hookset.
- **Blue cat — fresh cut shad, especially in cold water:** size 5/0-8/0 circle hook,
  same slip-sinker rig, sized up from channel cat gear since blue cats run larger
  and the current they favor requires more weight to hold bottom.
- **Flathead — live bait ONLY (this is not optional per `species.yaml`):** size
  5/0-8/0 wide-gap live-bait hook (not circle — flathead strikes and runs hard, a
  wide-gap J-hook hooksets more reliably on the take), hooked through the back or
  lips of a live sunfish, bullhead, or large shiner so it stays lively and mobile —
  the struggle is the trigger. Fished on a heavy slip-sinker rig near cover; expect
  to fish after dark per the species' nocturnal pattern.

## Predator group (spinning/casting lure only) — walleye, pike, muskellunge

- **Walleye jig + minnow (cold water, early season):** size 1/16-1/8 oz jighead
  (weight scales with wind/depth, not really a "hook size" choice) with a plain
  bait-holder-style hook built into the jig, tipped with a live minnow, fished on a
  lift-fall retrieve.
- **Walleye spinner harness / crawler rig (warming water):** size 2-4 octopus hooks
  (often two, in tandem) built into a pre-tied harness with beads/blades ahead of
  it, baited with a nightcrawler or leech, trolled or pulled slowly along bottom.
- **Pike/musky bucktail or spinnerbait:** the lure's own hook (typically a single
  4/0-6/0 or a treble in that size range) — not a separate rig, but ALWAYS use a
  wire or heavy fluorocarbon leader (12-18" minimum) ahead of it. Pike and musky
  teeth cut straight through mono/braid without one — this is the single most
  common gear mistake for this species group and it costs the fish and the lure.
- **Pike/musky live/dead bait under a float (a legitimate "sit and wait" option,
  not just artificials):** size 4/0-6/0 quick-strike rig — two trebles positioned
  along the baitfish's body so the hookset connects regardless of where the strike
  grabs it — again, always on a wire leader.

## Trout (rainbow, brown, brook, cutthroat) — spinning/casting only

This skill doesn't cover fly fishing (see SKILL.md's EXPAND mode for what happens
if that's ever actually asked for), so everything below is spin/casting gear —
real, effective trout tackle, just not the technique most trout culture assumes.

- **Small inline spinner (Mepps/Panther Martin-style, all-purpose):** the lure's
  own single or treble hook, typically size 6-10 built into a 1/16-1/8 oz spinner —
  size and color to match what's actually active (small, natural/silver-and-gold
  for clear water; brighter/larger blade for stained water or bigger fish). This is
  the single most versatile spin-tackle trout presentation across all four species.
- **Spoon (Kastmaster/Little Cleo-style):** built-in single or treble, 1/16-1/4 oz,
  fished on a straight retrieve or with an erratic twitch — better than a spinner
  for covering water fast or working deeper pools/runs.
- **Bait under a slip float or split-shot rig (nightcrawler piece, salmon eggs,
  PowerBait-style dough for stocked water):** size 8-12 baitholder or bait-holder-
  style hook, light wire — this is the standard approach for stocked put-and-take
  water specifically, less effective for wild/wary fish in clear water.
- **Egg pattern/bead under a light slip-float rig (not a fly-tackle technique
  despite looking like one):** a soft bead or egg-imitation bait can be fished on
  spinning tackle with a small single hook below it — worth knowing since it
  bridges the gap toward what a fly angler would call "egg patterns" without
  actually requiring fly gear.

## Kokanee Salmon — troll/spin gear, not a bank-cast lure

Kokanee are almost always fished by trolling in open water (see species.yaml —
this is a stillwater species), not by casting to a spot. **Small is the rule** —
their forage is plankton, there's no baitfish profile to match.

- **Dodger or flasher + small hoochie (squid-imitation skirt) or wedding-ring
  spinner, tipped with a kernel of corn (often scented/dyed pink or white):** size
  8-12 single or small treble built into the lure, trolled slowly (0.7-1.2 mph)
  near the depth the fish are holding at (see species.yaml's temperature note —
  track the thermocline, not a fixed depth). The dodger/flasher does the attracting;
  the small hook/bait behind it does the hooking.
- **Spawning-run water:** many states specifically restrict gear (no snagging, bait
  restrictions) during the fall tributary spawning run to protect the run itself —
  confirm via `us-water-finder` before recommending anything for that window, don't
  default to the open-water trolling setup above.

## Cross-cutting rules

- **Any fish likely to be released gets a barbless or crimped-barb hook and, where
  the species supports it, a circle hook** — reduces handling damage and deep-
  hooking risk. This matters most for muskellunge specifically, given the
  documented post-release mortality risk above 79°F (`species.yaml`), and for
  westslope cutthroat trout, where a mixed-species water often carries
  conservation-motivated catch-and-release expectations even where not legally
  required — check `us-water-finder` for the specific water's regulations, but
  default to barbless for cutthroat water when it isn't clear either way.
- **Leader material follows teeth, not species prestige.** Anything with real teeth
  (pike, musky, and to a lesser extent walleye) needs wire or heavy fluorocarbon —
  this isn't optional gear-upgrade advice, it's the difference between landing the
  fish and not.
- **Hook size scales with bait size and target size class, not a fixed number per
  species** — the ranges above are typical starting points; go up for known trophy
  water, down for a finesse/pressured-fish situation.
